The path

One clinical decision,
step by step

Status: Reference architecture

The same path every governed decision takes, shown here in a clinical context.

Domain data may include clinical records, imaging, monitoring signals and other evidence used in clinical decision-making.

A model proposes — a stratification, a flag, a reading of monitoring data — and states what the proposal rests on.

The governed decision layer establishes what the proposal rests on, tests it against the relevant clinical mechanism rather than plausibility alone, and records what evidence would defeat it.

An identified clinical authority decides whether the proposal may become action. Model confidence does not confer that authority.

In this reference path, action may proceed only within the authorised boundary.

The outcome is evaluated against criteria agreed beforehand — including the cases where nothing changed.

This is a reference path, not a description of a deployed clinical system.

One set of questions

Four questions before
anything acts

Every governed decision passes the same test, in this domain as in the other two.

Question 01

Verified premises

Are the premises verified or assumed?

In healthcare: are the clinical data and patient history complete, current and verified — or are missing facts being treated as known?

Question 02

Adequate mechanism

Does it hold against the domain’s own mechanism?

In healthcare: does the recommendation hold against the relevant clinical mechanism, not merely against a fluent explanation?

Question 03

Counter-evidence

What evidence would defeat it?

In healthcare: what finding would overturn the recommendation — and has that counter-evidence been actively sought?

Question 04

Acceptance authority

Who signs before anything acts?

In healthcare: who is the identified clinical authority that accepts the decision — and does the record show what was known at the time?

Current offering

What we can design
and build here

In healthcare, we design and build the governed decision layer on top of the systems and data infrastructure an organisation already trusts — with provenance, acceptance authority and evaluation — without asking it to replace them.

Within that layer, a model's proposal is grounded, tested against counter-evidence and presented to an identified clinical authority before any high-stakes action is taken.

Our role is to design and build the architecture and governance around the decision — not to diagnose, treat or assume clinical authority. Clinical decisions remain with the authorised clinician and institution.

Evidence

Research-led.
Transparently documented.

Status: Peer-reviewed research

Four peer-reviewed journal articles co-authored by our CTO were produced within the authors’ own institutions at the time, before and independently of SINAIGROUP-AI. They cover brain-volume change in primary-progressive multiple sclerosis, a finger-dexterity biosensing application, and two studies of COVID-19 vaccination in patients with multiple sclerosis.

Status: Preprint

Two preprints on medical digital twins and one on human digital twins develop the governance principles used here. Across them, model confidence is separated from authority to act, and inferred attributes are required to remain contestable.

Preprints document active research. They are not peer-reviewed validation of a product, and none of these records reports a clinical deployment.

Status: Prior founder experience

Before SINAIGROUP-AI, CTO Emanuel Shirbint worked in data analytics and data-warehouse architecture across healthcare and other sectors, and led research projects in neuroimmunology and medical imaging. This is prior founder experience, not a SINAIGROUP-AI delivery.

SINAIGROUP-AI currently has no healthcare deployments and no healthcare clients. The evidence presented here consists of prior founder experience and published research, not a SINAIGROUP-AI clinical delivery record.

Independence

Economics

Governed AI must produce
measurable value

Baseline

What happens today? What the current process costs or produces, established before anything is built.

Target

What measurable outcome should change? Agreed in advance, in the organisation’s own units, not in generic percentages.

Evaluation

Did it change, and what was it worth? Measured against the baseline after the pilot, including the cases where it did not.
