The decision boundary

Detection is not the same
as authority to intervene

A fraud score, rule hit or agent recommendation can support a decision without becoming the decision itself. The control layer should distinguish signal generation from routing, customer contact, account restriction, transaction intervention and other consequential actions.

That separation makes it possible to define which steps are automatic, which require escalation and which authority is accountable when the action affects a customer, merchant, transaction or operational state.

Six questions

Before a fraud signal
becomes an action

01

What evidence supports it?

Which transactions, events, attributes or external facts materially support the proposed intervention.

02

What could defeat it?

Which evidence would reduce confidence, identify a false positive or require a different action.

03

How current is the evidence?

Whether material status, identity, transaction or account information is current enough to rely on.

04

Who may intervene?

The person, role or pre-authorised policy boundary that permits the action.

05

What can be automated?

The exact intervention boundary, including tool access, value, duration, destination and rollback where available.

06

Can it be reconstructed?

Whether the decision record preserves enough evidence to explain what happened later.
