Platform overview

A 10-layer
architecture

Our AI-Powered Smart Infrastructure reference architecture is organised as ten blocks — eight functional layers that carry data from devices on the ground to AI in the cloud, and two cross-cutting planes that span all eight. It specifies how data is collected and analysed in real time, how predictive insight is generated, and how responses are proposed back to operations under human authority, with security and interoperability designed in throughout. Each block is set out below.

01

Layer 1 – IoT Edge Devices & Sensors

Industrial-grade sensors, smart meters, and controllers deployed on physical assets form the foundation. These devices continuously capture real-time data (e.g. temperature, vibration, pressure, flow) from equipment and environments, acting as the 'digital eyes and ears' of operations. By instrumenting turbines, pumps, pipelines, trucks and factory machines, we create a rich stream of data at the source that feeds into higher layers.

02

Layer 2 – Connectivity & Network Infrastructure

A robust communication layer links the edge devices to central systems. We utilize industrial Ethernet on sites, secure wireless links (LTE/5G), and reliable IoT protocols (MQTT, OPC-UA, etc.) to transmit data in real time. This connectivity is designed with built-in encryption and cybersecurity, ensuring data flows securely with minimal latency across widespread facilities.

03

Layer 3 – Edge Computing & Control

At facility sites, edge computing nodes (industrial PCs, PLCs, or gateways) process data locally and interface with control systems. They can filter sensor data, run fast anomaly checks, and execute automatic control logic on the spot. For instance, if a vibration reading spikes beyond a threshold, an edge device can trigger an immediate local alarm or adjustment without waiting for cloud input. This layer augments existing SCADA/DCS controls by adding AI-driven responsiveness on-site, improving safety and reducing latency.

04

Layer 4 – Data Ingestion & Integration

All field data is funneled into a unified data pipeline. The platform aggregates streams from sensors, edge devices, and existing enterprise systems into a central data repository. We implement high-speed data ingestion with filtering and normalization, so data from disparate sources (legacy databases, IoT feeds, etc.) can be analyzed together. Crucially, we integrate with your existing IT systems – maintenance management, ERP, GIS, and others – via APIs. This means IoT sensor data can be correlated with maintenance logs or weather info, breaking down silos and providing rich context for analysis.

05

Layer 5 – Data Storage & Management

A scalable data lake and warehouse store the collected data. High-volume time-series data (sensor readings, events) is stored alongside structured business data in a secure cloud-based repository. This single source of truth allows historical analysis and big-data analytics. We apply data governance, quality checks, and time synchronization to ensure the data is reliable and consistent. Years of operational data can be retained and mined for trends – for example, to discover patterns that precede a failure or to benchmark performance across sites.

06

Layer 6 – Analytics & AI Engine

At the core of the platform is the analytics layer where advanced algorithms turn raw data into actionable insights. We deploy machine learning models, statistical analyses, and AI algorithms to detect anomalies, predict failures, and optimize operations. For example, predictive models analyse sensor trends to indicate developing faults ahead of failure and recommend maintenance before a breakdown occurs; the available lead time varies by asset, instrumentation and failure mechanism. We also use techniques like digital twins (virtual models of assets) to simulate and diagnose complex scenarios. This layer enables the shift from reactive to proactive operations by answering: What is happening? Why? What's likely to happen next?

07

Layer 7 – User Interface & Dashboards

All insights are delivered through intuitive dashboards and visualization tools. We provide role-based interfaces for everyone from control room operators to executives. For instance, an operations dashboard might show real-time health scores for assets across all your plants, flagging any equipment in red that needs attention. Managers can see high-level KPIs while engineers can drill into detailed analytics. This ensures information is accessible and actionable at every level of the organization.

08

Layer 8 – Automation & Control Feedback

Beyond visual dashboards, the platform can 'close the loop' by proposing optimized control actions and routing them back to equipment through the existing control systems. For example, it can recommend opening a cooling valve or re-routing power load to prevent an overheating condition, presenting the supporting evidence to the operator. High-impact actions require approval by an identified human authority; where a narrow class of low-risk actions is pre-authorised, its boundaries, fail-safes, rollback path and emergency override are agreed in writing beforehand. Human oversight and fail-safes are maintained throughout.

09

Layer 9 – Security, Governance & Compliance (cross-cutting plane, Layers 1–8)

Underpinning every layer is a strong focus on cybersecurity and data governance. Industrial infrastructure is mission-critical, so our platform employs robust authentication, encryption, network security monitoring, and anomaly detection to guard against cyber threats. Data governance policies control access to sensitive data, address privacy, and are designed to support the regulatory regime that applies in your sector — electric grid cybersecurity standards or water safety requirements, for example. This plane also covers system health monitoring, so that the behaviour of models and services is observable rather than assumed. SINAIGROUP-AI holds no certification against these standards at this time.

10

Layer 10 – Integrative & Scalable Architecture (cross-cutting plane, Layers 1–8)

Our multi-layer design is highly integrative and flexible. It can plug into your existing equipment and systems, bridging formerly isolated OT and IT worlds into one cohesive whole. It's built to be cloud-agnostic and modular – running on major cloud providers or on-premises as needed, using containerization and microservices to avoid vendor lock-in. The architecture is designed to scale from a single facility to a nationwide network, and to support multi-cloud deployment for redundancy. In short, these ten blocks are a blueprint for smart infrastructure: eight layers carrying data from connected sensors through to decisions and back to governed action, and two planes that hold across all of them.

Key Platform Benefits

This layered structure is what makes the architecture modular, interoperable and extensible. It is designed to integrate with legacy and modern systems alike, giving one environment for data and control. It is cloud-agnostic — targeting AWS, Azure, Google Cloud or hybrid on-premises — using containerised microservices for portability and to avoid lock-in. It is designed to scale from a pilot to an enterprise-wide rollout, and to support multi-cloud deployment for failover. Because it is built on open standards, it can be extended as technologies and threats change. This ten-block method is the framework our team applies; where team members have applied comparable methods, those engagements are described, and attributed, in the Team section.

Sectors we serve

Where we make
an impact

Our expertise in AI-powered infrastructure spans a range of industrial and utility sectors. We know each sector has unique operational challenges and strategic goals. By combining our versatile platform with deep domain knowledge, we tailor solutions to deliver maximum impact in the following areas:

01

Power Generation & Utilities

For power producers (thermal, hydro, renewable) and electric utilities, the architecture targets plant performance, reliability and flexibility. It supports predictive maintenance of generation assets such as turbines, boilers and generators — analysing vibration, temperature and other sensor data to indicate developing faults ahead of an unplanned outage. A model that detects an incipient turbine-bearing issue, for instance, gives time to intervene before a catastrophic failure. It also supports performance optimisation: using machine learning to tune a boiler’s combustion in real time, where small heat-rate gains carry material annual fuel and emissions value. And it supports grid integration — forecasting demand and renewable output so that plants can ramp deliberately and hold stability. The aim in the energy sector is more reliable, more efficient and cleaner generation, with longer life from high-value equipment.

02

Transmission & Distribution (Smart Grids)

For electric transmission and distribution operators, we focus on asset longevity and grid intelligence. We deploy IoT sensors and analytics on critical grid equipment – transformers, switchgears, substations – to continuously monitor their health. Using condition-monitoring models of the kind documented in published utility asset-health programmes, the system is designed to detect early warning signs in transformers — abnormal dissolved gas levels or vibration patterns, for example — ahead of failure. This allows utilities to replace or service units before outages occur, preventing blackouts and extending equipment life. Analytics on load data from smart meters and line sensors can identify unusual losses, including possible theft, supporting a utility’s own loss-reduction programme. We also enable smarter grid operations: our platform assists in fault detection and dynamic grid control – for example, automatically isolating a faulty line and re-routing power, or pre-emptively adjusting capacitor banks and voltage regulators to maintain stability during peak loads. In essence, we bring advanced monitoring and control to T&D networks, making them more resilient, efficient, and 'smart' – key as the industry moves toward modern smart grids.

03

Manufacturing & Industry 4.0

We partner with heavy industry, discrete manufacturing, and process plant clients to drive the Industry 4.0 transformation on their factory floors. Our solutions turn traditional factories into smart factories by connecting machinery and applying AI to optimize operations. For example, we instrument production equipment (CNC machines, assembly robots, conveyors) with sensors to monitor their condition and performance. This enables predictive maintenance in manufacturing – catching early signs of machine wear or quality issues so maintenance can be performed during planned downtime, dramatically reducing unexpected line stoppages. Beyond maintenance, we use data analytics to improve throughput and quality. By analyzing production data, our platform can identify bottlenecks and recommend process tweaks that increase output. Where bottlenecks are the constraint rather than machine availability, the same instrumentation supports throughput analysis and process adjustment. We also help manufacturers implement digital twin simulations of their operations to optimize scheduling and supply chain logistics. The result is a more efficient, flexible, and intelligent manufacturing process that drives down costs and improves competitiveness.

04

Water & Public Utilities

In water supply, wastewater treatment, and similar public utilities, reliability and efficiency are paramount. Our CTO’s prior work, in a role held before SINAIGROUP-AI, included AI platform architecture for a national water utility; that experience is attributed to him in the Team section. This includes network-wide pressure and flow monitoring with AI analytics to detect leaks or pipe stress before a burst occurs. For example, the architecture provides for pressure-anomaly forecasting and operator alerting, so that a developing fault can be addressed before it becomes a burst. We also provide tools for risk modeling in water networks – segmenting pipeline systems into risk zones and running simulations (what-if scenarios like pump failures or fire demand spikes) to improve contingency planning. On the operations side, our SCADA modernization greatly benefits water utilities by uniting treatment plants, pump stations, and reservoirs under one control system for 24/7 oversight and optimization. We tackle energy efficiency in this sector too (pumping water is energy-intensive): our AI can optimize pump schedules and speeds to minimize energy use while meeting water demand, cutting costs and supporting sustainability initiatives. Overall, we help water and public utility clients deliver more reliable service (fewer outages, better water quality) and operate more cost-effectively, using data to drive proactive maintenance and efficient operations.

05

Healthcare Facilities

The same predictive, connected-infrastructure approach extends to hospital building systems and critical plant, where continuity of supply is a patient-safety matter. This is an emerging area for us: our healthcare experience to date is research, in medical imaging and neuroimmunology, and is listed in the R&D section. We have no healthcare infrastructure deployments.

06

Other Infrastructure Sectors

Thanks to our platform's flexible design, we can extend solutions to other sectors like transportation (e.g. rail systems, smart traffic management) and oil & gas (pipelines and refineries). The common theme is harnessing IoT data and AI insights to improve uptime, safety, and efficiency of complex, distributed assets. We are continually exploring new sectors where smart infrastructure can make an impact. Regardless of industry, we bring the same rigor in understanding domain-specific needs and customizing our approach to deliver maximum value.

Prior experience & reference scenarios

Where this
has been applied

The first two entries below describe work carried out by members of our team in roles held before SINAIGROUP-AI was formed; they are shown as professional background, not as corporate deliveries by SINAIGROUP-AI. The third is a reference scenario — how this architecture would be applied to a problem of that shape. Client names are withheld for confidentiality, and no outcome figures are published without the client’s written agreement.

01

Prior experience 1: AI-driven predictive maintenance at a national water utility

Challenge

Frequent pipeline bursts and leaks were occurring without warning, leading to service outages. The utility lacked early warning capabilities.

Solution

The programme deployed IoT pressure sensors across the network, with machine-learning models analysing the pressure data to give an early-warning system for pressure anomalies. The models forecast pressure trends and surfaced subtle patterns indicating developing problems, alerting operators ahead of potential pipe failures.

Outcome

The utility moved from reactive repair to risk-prioritised inspection: pipeline segments were clustered by risk so that inspection effort went where it was most likely to matter. Quantified outcomes belong to the client and are not published here. What the programme demonstrates is the method — that pressure analytics can reorder maintenance around risk rather than around failure.

02

Prior experience 2: nationwide SCADA modernisation for real-time operations

Challenge

The organization's control was fragmented – local stations had limited oversight and coordination from headquarters. Response to issues was slow and inefficient.

Solution

The programme replaced fragmented local control with a single modern SCADA system spanning the utility’s pump stations and wells, linked over a secure network to a new national control centre. Operators gained round-the-clock centralised visibility of the whole network, with alerts for pump failures, pressure drops and similar events on a real-time dashboard.

Outcome

Interventions that had required a site visit could be handled from the control centre on live data. The modernisation also laid the groundwork for analytics: operational data streamed into a cloud database where trends and performance could be examined across the network, giving the utility one authoritative operational record. It is a worked example of bringing legacy control systems into an OT/IT architecture — the pattern the ten blocks above describe.

03

Reference scenario: cloud migration of enterprise systems for integrated analytics

Challenge

The system handled critical business logic and reporting for operations, but was hampered by siloed data and limited scalability. Operational data in SAP was separate from IoT/SCADA data, making unified analysis impossible. Performance bottlenecks in the legacy reporting environment hindered timely insights.

Solution

The pattern: move the SAP data warehouse and reporting stack to a cloud platform, using SAP extraction tooling and cloud ETL pipelines so that historical and real-time data migrate with minimal disruption to operations. In the cloud, a unified data lake also ingests sensor and SCADA data, giving one integrated repository. Reporting is reimplemented on cloud BI tools, which is what makes enterprise and operational data analysable together.

Outcome

What the pattern buys: reporting latency falls, because the constraint moves from a fixed on-premises reporting tier to elastic compute. Enterprise financial data and operational sensor data become analysable on the same dashboard. Models that were previously infeasible — correlating sensor readings against maintenance logs to anticipate equipment failure — become possible because the two datasets finally sit together. IT overhead falls, since updates and scaling are handled by the platform rather than by staff.

forward-looking research

Generative AI & Knowledge Systems

Open Standards & Interoperability

Technological innovation isn't just about new algorithms – it's also about ensuring everything works together seamlessly. We embrace open standards and interfaces to maximize interoperability. Our solutions use protocols like OPC-UA for industrial connectivity, MQTT for messaging, and REST/GraphQL APIs for data exchange. The architecture provides for connectors to major industry systems — SAP, Oracle, Maximo and common SCADA platforms among them — to shorten integration work and avoid vendor lock-in. This commitment to open tech means clients can adopt our platform without scrapping what they already have – we meet you where you are. It also protects the investment, since new technology plugs into an open architecture more readily than into a closed one. By keeping interfaces open, we protect you from being tied to a single vendor or a dead-end system, and we enable a more flexible, long-term digital strategy.

03

Cloud & Edge Innovation

We leverage the best of cloud computing while also pushing intelligence to the edge. Our platform is designed to be cloud-agnostic and multi-cloud – it can run across AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, or private clouds in a distributed fashion. We use containerization (Docker, Kubernetes) to make our applications portable and scalable. A multi-cloud configuration the architecture supports: IoT device management on Azure, bulk storage on AWS and model training on GCP, unified under one control plane. This architecture ensures no single cloud vendor dependency and offers resilience: if one cloud has an outage, workloads can shift to another.

On the edge side, we work with the latest edge AI devices and IIoT gateways to enable local intelligence. We can deploy AI models on devices like NVIDIA Jetson at remote sites, allowing real-time analysis (even video/image analytics) on-site when latency or connectivity is a concern. Our edge solutions can perform complex analytics right at the source – for example, detecting a safety hazard via computer vision on a factory floor camera and alerting operators instantly, without needing to send all video to the cloud. By innovating in both cloud and edge, we create architectures that are highly responsive, reliable, and efficient.

We implement advanced cybersecurity measures – from anomaly-based intrusion detection to AI models that predict and flag abnormal network behavior – to stay ahead of emerging threats. Our platform's security layer is continually updated with best practices from both IT and OT security domains. Reliability is treated the same way: the design includes predictive failover, rerouting data or switching to backup systems on early indications of hardware fault or network trouble. Pilot program & roadmap

A phased
implementation roadmap

Modernizing infrastructure is a journey. We typically start with a focused pilot program to prove value on a small scale before scaling up. Our implementation roadmap is divided into clear phases with milestones, ensuring a smooth adoption of our technology. A typical engagement proceeds as follows:

01

Phase 1: Scoping & Infrastructure Setup (Months 0–3)

We work with you to identify an ideal pilot scope – for example, selecting representative sites or equipment for each core area (e.g. one power unit, one manufacturing line, one substation). We conduct site surveys to assess existing instrumentation and control systems. During this phase, we install any necessary IoT sensors and edge devices on the pilot assets (for instance, vibration sensors on a turbine, environmental sensors in a plant) and establish the connectivity backbone. By the end of Phase 1, the foundational hardware and network (platform Layers 1–3) are in place and tested for reliable data flow.

02

Phase 2: Data Integration & Model Development (Months 3–6)

We begin streaming data from the pilot assets into our central platform (activating Layers 4–5) and also integrate historical data from your databases (maintenance logs, SCADA archives) to enrich the AI's training set. Our experts work closely with your domain engineers to develop and fine-tune analytics models (Layer 6) for the pilot use cases. For example, we might create a predictive model for a boiler in the thermal plant and an anomaly detector for a transformer at the substation. This phase is highly iterative: as data comes in, models are trained and validated against known events (using historical failure data to ensure accuracy). We also provide hands-on training to your team on configuring the platform and interpreting early results. By the end of Phase 2, the platform is generating initial alerts and insights (with ongoing tuning), and your staff is gaining familiarity with the tools.

03

Phase 3: Pilot Rollout & Operationalization (Months 6–12)

We deploy the platform's applications and dashboards (Layers 7–8) to the pilot site personnel. This means control room operators, maintenance crews, and managers start using the new system, supported by workshops and training sessions. The platform runs in parallel with normal operations – providing recommendations, flags, and visualizations in real-time. During this period, we closely monitor performance and gather user feedback. We adjust any thresholds, model parameters, or UI features to improve usability and accuracy. Where you choose to enable the automation layer (Layer 8) for a narrow class of low-risk actions, the envelope is agreed in writing first — boundaries, fail-safes, rollback path and emergency override — and confidence is built inside it, for example by allowing a process setpoint to be tuned automatically and observing the outcome. Regular review meetings are held to measure pilot progress against predefined KPIs (e.g. number of detected anomalies, false alarm rate, maintenance interventions triggered). By the end of Phase 3, the platform is actively aiding operations, and your team is comfortable using it in their day-to-day workflow.

04

Phase 4: Evaluation & Scale-Up Planning (Month 12+)

After around a year of pilot operation, we conduct a comprehensive evaluation. We analyze the data collected to quantify improvements: e.g. reduction in downtime, cost savings, efficiency gains – the KPIs that were tracked. We jointly assess the ROI realized in the pilot versus expectations. If targets are met or exceeded, we then develop a roadmap for scaling the solution across your organization. This includes identifying priority sites or systems for the next rollout (typically those with the highest impact or ROI potential). We also explore the operational model for a broader deployment – for instance, establishing a Center of Excellence within your company to support the ongoing expansion of the platform. Additionally, success stories from the pilot can be compiled into internal case studies to build buy-in among stakeholders and even used externally to demonstrate your innovation leadership. By the end of Phase 4, we have a clear plan to transition from pilot to full implementation, ensuring lessons learned are applied for a smooth scale-up.

05

Change Management Throughout

Throughout all phases, change management is a key focus. We engage with your workforce to ensure they see the platform as an enabler, not a threat. We provide management and staff training at all levels to foster a data-driven culture in parallel with the technical rollout. Regular communication, quick wins, and involvement of end-users in the process help build confidence and adoption. The phased pilot approach not only demonstrates ROI early but also allows the solution to be refined iteratively, increasing the chances of success at full scale.

06

Summary

In summary, our pilot program de-risks the digital transformation journey. By starting small, proving value, and then scaling deliberately, SINAIGROUP-AI works so that AI-powered infrastructure projects deliver real results and gain the trust of your team. We walk with you through every step – from concept to full production – as partners in innovation.

ROI & KPI outcomes

Measurable
value

SINAIGROUP-AI works to measurable value: success is defined in operational and financial terms, agreed in advance. The indicators below are the outcome targets we would set and track with you, and the categories in which value is normally found. They are objectives and measurement definitions — not results SINAIGROUP-AI has recorded.

01

Unplanned Downtime Reduction

The first target of predictive maintenance and real-time monitoring is unplanned equipment downtime. The baseline is set from your own outage history, and the target reduction is agreed against that baseline before the pilot rather than asserted in advance of it. This higher asset availability translates directly into increased production/revenue (each day of uptime for a 500 MW power plant is significant generation output). In utilities and manufacturing, fewer breakdowns also mean improved service reliability and on-time delivery.

02

Maintenance Cost Savings

Moving from reactive fixes to proactive maintenance changes where maintenance money goes. Avoiding catastrophic failure and emergency repair removes damage cost and overtime labour; optimised schedules extend asset life and reduce spare-parts inventory. These are the line items we would measure with you, from your own maintenance ledger, so that any saving claimed at the end of a pilot is one you can trace in your accounts.

03

Efficiency & Productivity Gains

In thermal generation we would target a 1–2% improvement in heat rate. That sounds small and is not: heat rate multiplies against every hour of fuel burned, so the annual value scales with plant size, load factor and fuel price, and we model it for your plant before agreeing the target. In manufacturing, the equivalent targets are throughput and labour productivity, set from your current line performance. The principle in both cases is the same — more output for the same input, measured against a baseline you already hold.

04

Safety & Compliance Improvements

Reducing unexpected failures also improves safety, as there are fewer incidents of catastrophic breakdowns. Early warning of issues allows maintenance to be planned under controlled conditions rather than in emergency, high-risk situations. Monitoring is also designed to surface deviations from regulatory thresholds — emissions levels, water quality metrics — early enough to act on them, which supports your own compliance reporting rather than replacing it.

05

Energy Savings & Sustainability

Our optimizations frequently reduce energy consumption and associated emissions. Efficiency improvement and smarter scheduling reduce fuel burned per unit of output, and therefore emissions per unit of output; in water networks, pump-schedule optimisation targets the same effect on electricity. Where a reduction is quantified, we state the calculation basis and the boundary alongside the number. These gains help clients meet sustainability targets (such as greenhouse gas reduction goals) while also cutting utility bills. We can quantify metrics like kWh saved, tons of CO₂ avoided, and improvements in carbon intensity of operations, providing data for your ESG reporting.

06

Service Reliability & Customer Impact

For utilities (power, water, etc.), our solutions translate to better service uptime for end customers. Fewer outages and faster incident response improve customer satisfaction ratings and reduce compensation payouts for service disruptions. Customer-impacting incidents are the indicator that matters most in this category, and it is the one we would track year on year from your own incident register. In public infrastructure, smarter systems mean cities experience less downtime in transit systems, and citizens enjoy more reliable infrastructure services.

07

Commitment to Transparency

Every project defines specific KPIs at the outset – whether it's a percentage reduction in failures, megawatt-hours of capacity added, or dollars saved per year. We track these diligently throughout implementation. Our commitment is that a SINAIGROUP-AI engagement is judged on ROI, not only on whether the technology works. We build the ROI model with you at the outset and track it through the pilot; payback depends on your asset base, your baseline performance and the scope agreed. We believe in sharing these success metrics transparently. We structure pilots around a small number of high-value objectives agreed in advance and measurable at the end, so that the case for scale-up is made from evidence rather than from enthusiasm.

08

Strategic Alignment

By focusing on key outcomes – reliability, efficiency, cost, safety, and sustainability – we design smart infrastructure projects to align with your top-line and bottom-line goals. ROI isn't an afterthought; it's our starting point. We design and implement with the end results in mind, so that your investment in innovation translates into tangible performance improvements and competitive advantage.

01

MCKINSEY: AI, GOVERNANCE & INFRASTRUCTURE

MCKINSEY RESEARCH · INDUSTRY EVIDENCE

From AI experimentation to governed infrastructure value

Across its recent research, McKinsey identifies a consistent pattern: AI adoption is accelerating, but capturing measurable value at scale depends on reliable data, effective governance and integration with real operating workflows.

Nearly two-thirds of enterprises worldwide have experimented with AI agents, yet fewer than 10% have scaled them to deliver tangible value. Eight in ten cite data limitations as a barrier to scale.

Governance represents another major constraint. In McKinsey’s 2026 AI Trust Maturity Survey, nearly two-thirds of respondents identified security and risk as the leading barrier to fully scaling agentic AI. Seventy-four percent cited inaccuracy and 72% cybersecurity as highly relevant risks, while only about 30% reported maturity level three or higher in strategy, governance and agentic AI controls.

In asset-intensive infrastructure, the potential benefits are measurable. McKinsey reports that advanced analytics–enabled predictive maintenance can support typical yearly savings of 10–20% in maintenance operating expenditure and 20–30% in capital expenditure for water utilities when implemented effectively.

For public-sector infrastructure investments, McKinsey estimates that digital twins have the potential to improve capital efficiency, service accessibility and operational performance by 20–30%.

In manufacturing environments, McKinsey reports that predictive maintenance typically reduces machine downtime by 30–50% and increases machine life by 20–40%.

Taken together, these findings indicate that enterprise AI value does not come from isolated models alone. It depends on governed data foundations, operational integration, continuous monitoring and clearly accountable decision processes.

The architecture we build on

Governed AI
execution

Our platforms are built on one governed architecture: agents that take real action, but never on their own authority. High-impact actions are committed by an identified human; where a narrow class of low-risk actions is pre-authorised, its boundaries, fail-safes, rollback path and emergency override are agreed in writing beforehand. Everything is grounded, evaluated and traceable.

01
Human Acceptance Authority
The AI proposes an action; an identified human commits it. No self-approval on anything critical.
02
Grounded in Your Data
Agents act on your systems and your data — connected to SCADA, ERP, historians and more.
03
Full Provenance
Every decision and state is specified to be recorded and traceable end to end, with SHA-256 hashing of the provenance chain.
04
Bounded & Auditable Change
The platform improves in controlled, reversible steps — never an opaque black box.
05
Adversarial Evaluation
Evaluation against disconfirming evidence and adversarial pressure before anything is trusted — the method is set out in our published research.
06
Security & Observability
Monitored and secured across every layer, edge to cloud, and designed to support the compliance regime that applies to your sector.
Sound, not just fluent

Built for
high-stakes decisions

A fluent answer is not a safe one. In critical infrastructure and healthcare, our architecture is designed to enforce sound reasoning — the framework is set out in our published research on sound abduction — so that a confident-sounding recommendation is also a defensible one.

01
Verified Premises
Required facts are verified — or explicitly flagged as unknown, never quietly assumed.
02
Adequate Mechanism
Every recommendation must hold up against a constrained domain model, not just sound plausible.
03
Counter-Evidence Handling
Conflicting evidence can down-rank, suspend or defeat a conclusion — not get ignored.
04
Human Approval Gates
A person signs off before any high-stakes action, with the full reasoning trail attached.
