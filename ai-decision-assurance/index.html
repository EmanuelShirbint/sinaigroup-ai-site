The problem

Capability is not
authority to act

A model may be accurate. An agent may be capable. A recommendation may be plausible. None of those facts, by themselves, establish that a consequential action should proceed.

Decision assurance asks whether the proposed action is supported by adequate evidence, whether important premises were verified, what counter-evidence should stop or change the decision, who has authority to accept it, and whether the resulting action stays inside an explicit boundary.

The objective is not another AI answer. The objective is a decision that can be inspected before action and reconstructed afterwards.

Five questions

Before anything
consequential acts

Question 01

What supports the decision?

Identify the evidence that materially supports the proposed action and its provenance.

Question 02

What could defeat it?

Specify counter-evidence, missing information and failure conditions that should suspend, downgrade or overturn the recommendation.

Question 03

Who has authority?

Model confidence is not institutional authority. The accountable person, role or pre-authorised boundary must be explicit.

Question 04

What may the AI do?

Recommendation, drafting, escalation and execution are different levels of authority. The permitted action boundary makes that distinction operational.

Question 05

Can it be reconstructed?

The record should preserve what was known, what was proposed, what challenged it, who accepted it, what action followed and what outcome occurred.

The decision path

From intelligence
to governed action

Status: Reference architecture

Evidence → the information available at the time and its provenance.

Model / AI / Agent → the recommendation, classification, prioritisation or proposed action.

Premise verification → checks on facts the recommendation depends on.

Counter-evidence → observations that could invalidate, qualify or reverse the proposal.

Evaluation → agreed criteria used to decide whether the proposal is adequate.

Human Acceptance Authority → the identified authority that may accept, reject, escalate or stop the action when human accountability is required.

Permitted Action Boundary → what the AI may and may not do automatically.

Governed Decision Record → an inspectable record of the decision, authority and resulting action.

This is a reference architecture. It is not a regulatory certification, legal opinion, guarantee of compliance or guarantee of model performance.

What this is not

Not another model.

SINAIGROUP-AI works around systems an organisation already operates. Models remain models. Risk, clinical, engineering and business owners retain their authority.

FAQ

AI Decision Assurance
in plain language

What is AI Decision Assurance?

AI Decision Assurance examines the evidence, counter-evidence, authority, action boundaries and audit record surrounding a consequential AI-supported decision.

How is it different from model validation?

Model validation evaluates model behaviour and performance. Decision assurance examines the wider operational decision: its evidence, assumptions, counter-evidence, authority, permitted action and resulting record.

How is it different from AI governance?

AI governance defines organisational policies, responsibilities and acceptable use. Decision assurance examines how those expectations hold around an actual decision path.

What is Human Acceptance Authority?

Human Acceptance Authority identifies the person or role with authority to accept, reject, escalate or stop an AI-supported action where human accountability is required.

What should an AI decision record contain?

A useful record can include the evidence available at the time, relevant model or agent state, the proposed decision, counter-evidence, approval or rejection, authority exercised, resulting action and observable outcome.
