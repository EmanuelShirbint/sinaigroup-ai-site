What is at stake

The path

One financial decision,
step by step

Status: Reference architecture

The same path that every governed decision takes, read for a decision about capital, risk or an obligation.

Financial data — positions, transactions, market and reference data, and the records an institution already keeps.

A model or agent proposes — a risk it has identified, a pattern it has detected, a recommended action — and states what the proposal rests on.

The governed decision layer establishes the evidence behind the proposal, tests it against market structure rather than plausibility, and records what would defeat it.

An identified person with authority — or an explicit, pre-authorised boundary — decides whether the proposal may become action. Decision authority stays explicit.

Action is executed only within the boundary that was authorised.

The outcome is audited and evaluated against criteria agreed beforehand, so that the decision can be defended afterwards.

This is a reference path, not a description of a deployed system or of any client's process.

One set of questions

Four questions before
anything acts

Every governed decision passes the same test, in this domain as in the other two.

Question 01

Verified premises

Are the premises verified or assumed?

In financial services: a price, a rating or a counterparty status taken as current when it is stale.

Question 02

Adequate mechanism

Does it hold against the domain’s own mechanism?

In financial services: does the recommendation hold against how the market or the product actually behaves, not only against a fluent rationale?

Question 03

Counter-evidence

What evidence would defeat it?

In financial services: which observation would overturn the recommendation, and is it being watched for?

Question 04

Acceptance authority

Who signs before anything acts?

In financial services: the person accountable for the decision signs — and the record shows what was known, and what authority was exercised, at that moment.

Current offering

What we can design
and build here

We design and build the governed decision layer on top of the data and financial systems an institution already runs — provenance, acceptance authority and evaluation — without asking it to replace them.

Three things about a recommendation can be inspected: where its evidence came from, how it was evaluated against counter-evidence, and who accepted it.

The economic case is set before the work: baseline, target and evaluation in the institution's own units. Our CEO owns the ROI framework used to judge the engagement.

We do not manage capital or portfolios, execute trades, score credit, underwrite, or operate a bank's production systems, and we give no investment advice: the decision, and the accountability for it, stay with the institution.

Economics

Governed AI must produce
measurable value

Baseline

What happens today? What the current process costs or produces, established before anything is built.

Target

What measurable outcome should change? Agreed in advance, in the organisation’s own units, not in generic percentages.

Evaluation

Did it change, and what was it worth? Measured against the baseline after the pilot, including the cases where it did not.
