Why logs are not enough

A technical log is not
a governed decision record

A model response can be logged perfectly and still leave the organisation unable to answer the questions that matter: which evidence was available at the time, whether the premises were current, what counter-evidence was considered, who had authority, which action was actually executed and what happened afterwards.

An audit trail for consequential AI therefore has to join technical state with decision context and authority.

Minimum record

What should be
preserved

Evidence provenance

Source, timestamp, version and the evidence materially used by the decision.

System state

Relevant model, agent, prompt, policy, tool and configuration state.

Proposal

The recommendation, classification, prioritisation or proposed action.

Challenge

Premise checks, counter-evidence, uncertainty and failure conditions considered.

Authority

Who accepted, rejected, escalated or stopped the proposal, or which pre-authorised boundary applied.

Action & outcome

What changed, when it changed and the observable result that followed.

Design principle

Record the chain
as it happens

Reconstructability is strongest when the decision record is created as part of the workflow rather than assembled manually after an incident or review.

The record should be inspectable without implying that every data element must be duplicated into a new system. References, immutable identifiers, version binding and provenance can preserve the chain while source data remains in the systems that already own it.

The use case

Decision → action →
outcome

For model validation, risk oversight, claims, underwriting, fraud intervention, agentic workflows or another consequential process, the useful question is not only “what did the model say?” It is “what decision was taken, under what evidence and authority, what action followed and what outcome later became observable?”

That chain creates a basis for validation, incident review, governance oversight and learning.

FAQ

AI audit trails
in plain language

What is an AI audit trail?

A reconstructable record of the evidence, system state, proposed decision, checks, authority, action and outcome surrounding an AI-supported decision.

Why is logging model output not enough?

Because model output alone does not show which evidence was available, what assumptions were checked, who had authority, what downstream action occurred or what outcome followed.

What should an AI decision record contain?

Evidence provenance, relevant model or agent state, proposed decision, counter-evidence, evaluation, human approval or rejection, resulting action and observable outcome.
